How to read it

  1. The link branch (top) is the safe one: the user is logged in, recently, and asked for it.
  2. A known identity never looks at email. Only brand-new identities reach the email questions.
  3. The two yellow diamonds are where apps get taken over: auto-linking on an untrusted email claim, or linking to an account whose email was never verified.
  4. Every link sends an email to the account owner.

2. Data flow: linking with proof

Microsoft says "rahul@gmail.com" but PrintKaro doesn't trust Microsoft's email. Rahul proves it's him; Vikram can't.

sequenceDiagram
    autonumber
    actor V as Vikram
    actor R as Rahul
    participant P as PrintKaro
    participant M as Microsoft (mock)

    V->>P: POST /login/microsoft
    P->>M: authorize, then token + userinfo
    M-->>P: sub f00dcafe...0666, email = Rahul's email, not verified
    P-->>V: 409 account exists, log in with password to connect
    V->>P: POST /link/confirm
    P-->>V: 401 log in first (he has no password for Rahul's account)
    R->>P: POST /login/microsoft
    M-->>P: sub 9188040d...ea81, email = Rahul's email, not verified
    P-->>R: 409 account exists, pending link saved in this session
    R->>P: POST /login, his email + password
    P-->>R: 200 loggedIn acct_4SS_jw, pendingLink Microsoft, confirm?
    R->>P: POST /link/confirm
    P->>P: link microsoft and 9188040d...ea81 to acct_4SS_jw, email Rahul
    P-->>R: 200 identities google, github, microsoft

How to read it

  1. Each column is one part of the system. Solid arrows are requests, dashed arrows are replies.
  2. Steps 1 to 6: Vikram gets the same answer as Rahul, but can't do the next step.
  3. Step 9: the pending link is kept in Rahul's session and survives the login (step 10), which rotates the session id.
  4. Step 12: the link happens only after an explicit confirm.

3. Pre-account hijacking

Vikram signs up first with Priya's email. Left: a naive app. Right: PrintKaro.

sequenceDiagram
    autonumber
    actor V as Vikram
    participant A as Naive app
    actor Pr as Priya

    V->>A: sign up with Priya's email + HIS password
    A->>A: account X, email not verified
    Pr->>A: Log in with Google (verified)
    A->>A: same email, link Google to X
    Pr->>A: saves her address and card in X
    V->>A: logs in with his password
    A-->>V: account X, Priya's data

sequenceDiagram
    autonumber
    actor V as Vikram
    participant P as PrintKaro
    actor Pr as Priya

    V->>P: sign up with Priya's email + HIS password
    P->>P: account X, email not verified
    Pr->>P: Log in with Google (trusted, verified)
    P->>P: X is unverified, so never link, X loses the email
    P-->>Pr: her OWN new account
    V->>P: logs in with Priya's email + his password
    P-->>V: 401 wrong email or password

The rule: never link to an account whose email was never verified. When a trusted provider proves the email, the unverified account loses its claim and the real owner gets a clean account.
